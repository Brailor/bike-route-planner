# Spike: can GraphHopper route gravel in Austria?

- Date: 2026-10-29
- Engine: GraphHopper 11.1, Austria extract of 2026-10-28, models as in `services/routing/models/`
- Reproduce: `node services/routing/spikes/gravel-spike.mjs services/routing/data/spikes/gravel` (engine running).
  Writes `results.json` and one GPX per area × variant (gitignored).

## Question

Does GraphHopper, within the LM constraint (per-request custom models may only penalise, ADR 0002), produce
believable gravel routes? Or should we switch to BRouter (ADR 0001)?

## Setup

Five point-to-point routes in known gravel areas, each routed with these variants:

| Variant                      | Per-request custom model on top of the profile                                                     |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `road`                       | none (`road` profile)                                                                              |
| `gravel`                     | none (`gravel` profile = GraphHopper's `bike`)                                                     |
| `gravel-paved-{0.8,0.6,0.4}` | priority × f where the surface is tagged paved                                                     |
| `gravel-likely-{0.6,0.4}`    | priority × f where the way is _likely_ paved (see below)                                           |
| `gravel-likely-0.4-nonet`    | as above, and × 0.67 on likely-paved signed cycle routes (cancels `bike_priority`'s network boost) |
| `mtb`                        | none (`mtb` profile)                                                                               |

"Likely paved" = surface tagged paved, **or** surface missing on anything that is not a track/path/bridleway,
**or** surface missing on a `grade1` track. "Likely unpaved" (the metric) = tagged unpaved, or surface missing on a
track/path/bridleway that is not `grade1`.

## Findings

1. **The default `gravel` profile is a road profile.** It is 0–12% unpaved in all five areas. GraphHopper's bike
   speeds are 18 km/h on asphalt and 12 km/h on gravel, tracks and `grade2`. Since weight ≈ time / priority, every
   unpaved kilometre already costs 1.5× an asphalt one, and the priority boosts on good tracks don't make up for it.
2. **Penalising only _tagged_ paved surfaces backfires.** It moves the route onto roads with **no** surface tag,
   which in Austria are mostly paved. At `paved-0.4`, untagged non-track roads made up 36% of the Donau-Auen route
   and 28% of Mödling. A gravel preference has to infer "paved" for untagged ordinary roads and `grade1` tracks.
3. **Signed cycle routes need neutralising.** `bike_priority` boosts signed cycle routes by up to 1.5×. In the
   Leithagebirge that kept every variant on the signed, paved, main-road route (54–72% primary/secondary) until the
   boost was cancelled on likely-paved roads. With it cancelled, the route crosses the forest (77% likely unpaved, 0%
   main roads, +7% distance).
4. **With both fixes, the result is believable on paper:** 55–77% likely unpaved in every area, at 0.96–1.16× the
   road distance and ≤ 9% main roads. All factors are within [0, 1], so this works under LM without `lm.disable`.
5. **OSM data gaps are big.** Up to 74% of a gravel route (Donau-Auen) has no surface tag at all. The "likely
   unpaved" figure leans heavily on `highway=track` + `tracktype`. The surface breakdown in the UI must show
   "unknown" honestly, and user corrections (M2) matter.
6. **Gravel can mean much more climbing.** Mödling → Heiligenkreuz goes from 314 m to 594 m of ascent, because
   forest roads go over the hills rather than along the valley. The UI should make that trade-off visible.

## Verdict

**Keep GraphHopper. No switch to BRouter**, provided the ground-truth check below doesn't contradict the numbers.

## Implications for M1

- `buildCustomModel` for gravel/MTB: the off-road slider maps to a factor f ∈ [~0.3, 1] applied to the
  **likely-paved** expression, plus cancelling the cycle-network boost on likely-paved ways. The "less off-road" half
  of the slider applies the mirror penalty to likely-unpaved ways.
- Surface breakdown categories: paved / gravel / rough / **unknown**, with unknown tracks shown by `tracktype` where
  available.
- Rough surfaces (`ground`, `dirt`, `grass`) reach 18–22% in the Weinviertel at 0.4. Consider a separate "avoid rough"
  setting (the `-norough` variant shows the expression works).

## Open

- **Ground truth (needs local knowledge).** Compare the GPX files in `services/routing/data/spikes/gravel/gpx/`
  (`<area>--gravel-likely-0.4-nonet.gpx` vs `--road.gpx`) with what you know and with satellite imagery. Are those
  tracks rideable on a gravel bike, and are they the ones a local would pick?
- **Legality of forest roads.** Austrian forest law restricts cycling on forest roads unless explicitly permitted.
  GraphHopper's Austria country rule only covers motor vehicles. We haven't checked how the forest tracks used here
  are tagged (`bicycle=*`, `mtb=*`, `access=*`); an Overpass query timed out (504). Next step: read the tags of the
  route's track way IDs (in `results.json`) from the local `.pbf`.
- **Car profile (M4).** Car routing snapped onto a forest track in the Leithagebirge. Enable GraphHopper's
  `country_rules` (Austria marks tracks `FORESTRY` for motor vehicles) and penalise `road_access` in `car-model.json`.
